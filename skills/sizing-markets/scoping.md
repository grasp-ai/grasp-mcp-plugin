## Phase 1: the scoping session

The scope decides the whole analysis, so it is confirmed with the user before any number is searched for. A one-paragraph scope from the user is a starting point, not a scope: draw the grid, propose defaults for everything the user did not say, confirm in chat, then write it down with `grasp_set_market_sizing_scope`. Before confirmation, look up only what the grid itself needs, such as a named company's profile or how the industry segments itself. No market figures, no universe search, no report reading.

### The taxonomy grid

Two axes are always there: the offerings that make up the market, named as the industry names them, and the geography as a list of countries. Every other dimension is specific to the market and is yours to propose: customer segment or end market, an activity split, a value-chain position, a business model. Pick the ones along which this market's boundaries actually run, say why, and let the user add or drop dimensions in their own words. Mark every cell in, out or adjacent.

Adjacent cells matter more than they look. Targets often straddle them, and a wide company search will pull them in whether you want them or not, so they stay visible in every later deliverable rather than being quietly dropped or quietly included. Name each adjacent cell so that the researcher cannot widen it: a security cell is "installation of security systems", and the scope sentence says that guarding and alarm monitoring are out.

### What the scope fixes

Each setting has a default the user can accept or correct. State the defaults in the message; ask only about the ones the request left genuinely open.

- **Value-chain layer**: whose revenue counts. Manufacturer, distributor, installer, service provider or end-customer spend. One layer per sizing, recorded in `value_chain_layer`.
- **HQ rule**: whether companies headquartered outside the geography but operating inside it are counted. Recommend counting them, since a market is the revenue earned inside the geography; the audit places their revenue per country from their reports. HQ-only is the alternative, for a user who wants the domestic players rather than the market.
- **Basis**: gross revenue or net of pass-through. Pass-through is revenue one in-scope company books and pays to another in-scope company, such as an installer's subcontractors; counted gross it appears twice. Where the value chain has it, default to net, which needs a sourced share of revenue passed on; without one the total is reported gross and the page says so. Where it does not, report gross and say why.
- **Reference year and currency**: default to the last full calendar year, in the user's working currency unless the market reports in one local currency. A market spanning several currencies keeps the user's. State both, do not ask: neither is revisited later, and a filing from another year is restated to this one.
- **Size floor**: companies above a headcount floor are enumerated one by one; those below it are estimated as a tail. Default 10 employees.
- **Bottom-up shape**: how the company list is built, decided by the kind of market rather than by the list of countries. Say which shape you chose and why, and record it in `bottom_up_shape`.
  - Many local companies that file accounts, such as installers, contractors and service firms where filings are public: enumerate country by country, so each country gets a bottom-up of its own. A country is enumerated when its universe fits the search after splitting by sub-trade and filed revenue covers most of it; one where few companies file is carried by the top-down with the bottom-up as its cross-check. Record the enumerated countries in `enumerated_countries`.
  - A few makers or brands selling across borders, such as a product niche or any global scope: one player list for the whole geography, holding the specialists and the larger groups that sell a line in this market however small a part of their revenue it is. Their revenue sits where they are headquartered, not where the market buys, so the list checks the total rather than splitting it; the regional split comes from the top-down's published regional shares or a stated proxy.
- **Geographic resolution**: which geographies the page reports, decided by the question behind the sizing and by where the market's weight sits. A handful of countries is reported country by country. A wide scope names the countries or regions that matter, for a due diligence the target's own and for a whole-market view the largest, and puts the rest into one remainder such as "Rest of Europe", sized as one figure. Where the user asks for a region and parts of it, such as Europe and the Nordics and DACH within it, name the parts, put the region's other countries in a remainder such as "Rest of Europe" so the region is whole, and give each the region as its `group`; the page adds them up to the region. A remainder, including one for the scope countries the user's own list of geographies leaves out, is named for what it holds: the country's name when it is one country, a name such as "Rest of Europe" when several, never a description in brackets. The scope sentence says it holds the scope countries no other geography names, and where the user's list left countries out, your first message after the scope is set says which countries went there. Record them in `geographies` with every scope country in exactly one; leave it empty to report each country. With country enumeration, enumerate the named countries and leave the remainder's countries out of `enumerated_countries`, a remainder named after its one country included, since a figure of its own sizes it at the reconciliation; with a player list, the named geographies are splits of the headline.
- **The question behind the sizing**: it decides how deep the cuts go, which countries need a number of their own, and which parts of the market need a growth rate and drivers of their own. There are three, and in the message each option names one by what it changes in the result, fitted to the scope, such as "Whole market: size, regional split and growth", "A target's addressable market: the segments and countries it sells in, sized" and "Growth outlook for an investment case: drivers and forecast by segment". A one-country scope has no regional split to promise.
- **Anchors the user already holds**: a report, a figure a client quoted, a target's own market claim. Never ask for them, not even in an optional line. Record each one the user gives unasked in `user_anchors` with its label, and pass a figure that settles a later choice as the user's, where the tool that takes it asks for a source.

### Confirming

Confirm in one chat message laid out to scan. It leads with the grid as a table, so nothing the run searches by is first seen on the page: three columns, one naming the part of the market in its own words, one for what is in, and one for what sits at the edge or out, where each adjacent cell says whether it counts in the total or points to the question that asks about it. Its rows are the offerings, each other dimension and the geography, holding everything `grasp_set_market_sizing_scope` will record: every offering, the countries and the geographies they are reported as, every value of every other dimension, and every adjacent cell, each in two to four words, so a long list of offerings reads as their names. The defaults follow the table. Skip asking anything the request already answered and restate it instead.

The plan follows in one or two sentences: a full sizing takes about 15 to 45 minutes and runs on Grasp's side, this is its only stop, it uses research credits to research the companies in the market where the plan charges for them, and once the scope is confirmed the user can leave it running and come back. That confirmation is what the later tools run on, so say it plainly and don't ask again before each one. Then a short list says how the later choices will be made, in the market's words, so a user who would choose differently says so now:

- In a country sized company by company where few publish their revenue, the others are estimated from similar companies' revenue per employee, with a wider range and market sizes others have published as the check, unless an official statistic covers exactly this market there.
- Unless a source gives the share of revenue passed on to subcontractors, the total is reported before that share is taken out, and the page says so.
- A company the search misses counts in the total when its accounts give its revenue, and only in the top of the range when its revenue has to be estimated.
- A company on both sides of a boundary is counted by the rule the scope gives for that boundary.
- A country or region no company search reaches is sized from a market size published for it or a stated stand-in.

The message uses none of these instructions' working terms, such as "the list", "enumerated", "axis" or "the high case".

Once confirmed, call `grasp_set_market_sizing_scope` with the scope sentence, the activity, the value-chain layer, the countries and the geographies they are reported as, the currency, the basis, the reference year, the floor, the HQ rule, every adjacent cell with its in or out decision, and the purpose. The grid itself goes in too: the offering rows in `offerings` and every other axis you settled on in `dimensions`, as the industry names them. Those are the parts the growth search has to answer for, so an axis left out of the record is an axis nothing later reports on. They are also what the company research reads, word for word and in a fixed order, in place of the scope sentence, which is for people, so every run with the same grid draws the same boundary: name each offering, dimension value and adjacent cell so that a researcher reading only it knows what is in and what is out, and put the rule that decides a straddling company in the cell's note. Put the scope block at the top of every later deliverable.

If a boundary turns out to be wrong later, say a large player straddles two cells, place it by the rule on its cell and record that as a scope choice you made. A decision that changes what is searched is written with `grasp_set_market_sizing_scope` again.
