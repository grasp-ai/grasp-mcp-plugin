## Phase 4: reconcile and range

**The reconciliation table.** Call `grasp_reconcile_market_size`. The arithmetic is the tool's; your part is the judgement it asks for:

- **The pass-through factor.** Where the scope asked for net and the handover holds no sourced share, the output says so: the headline stays gross and the page names net as a gap. A share the user gave goes in with provenance `user`, attributed to them on the page. None is needed where a published base carries the headline.
- **The bases.** Default bottom-up everywhere, the headline under a player list included; a published figure for the same scope is its cross-check, and the gap between them goes in `gap_causes`. An enumerated country stays with its companies however much of it is modelled, unless a statistics office or regulator publishes a figure for exactly the scope in that country; that figure then carries it, with the companies as its cross-check. Otherwise a top-down figure carries only a country the scope left out of the enumeration, the third-party figure for it whose definition is closest to the scope, the most credible publisher first, or one the user asks for. Each such figure goes in `bases`: its figure as the source states it, the year it states it for, its label, publisher and source, the countries it covers, a name the page can use, and a note saying which leg it is, why the bottom-up does not carry those countries, and what the alternative would have been. A published base is taken on the scope's basis: in a net scope, a figure already net of pass-through, or one adjusted with the adjustment in its note. A derived base built from companies' revenue, such as the bottom-up plus the filed revenue of companies it leaves out, is gross: mark it `built_from_companies` and the tool nets it with the bottom-up. The bottom-up over the same countries becomes the base's cross-check, and the tool prints the two side by side. A base is a figure a helper read from a document, or a remainder built as below. A figure that states the market's own turnover for two or more years is also its history: where the growth record's rates over those years are more than a point a year off it, call `grasp_research_market_growth` once with a `focus` naming that series before reconciling, rather than growing the base by a rate of your own.
- **The top-down estimate.** Rebuild the market from the figures read, the way a consultant would, and pass it in `top_down`: one piece per segment of the split, or per geography the page reports where there is none. Each piece starts from the figure in the figures table that needs the fewest steps to reach it, a wider one included: a regional figure for one country, the whole market for one segment. Each step keeps a share of it in the low and the high case: a country's share of a region, a segment's share of the market, the in-scope activity's share of a trade, a haircut in the low case for a free summary of a paid report, an add-back in the high case for a part the figure leaves out. Cite each share from what the helpers recorded; where none was read, give the share you assume and why in one line. A piece with several credible reports takes a funnel from each, up to three, so the reader sees whether they agree. A piece no figure reaches keeps no funnel and says what was looked for; never fill it from the bottom-up, which the top-down exists to check. Each piece's confidence says how much of it rests on sources, with why in a line: high only where every start is a filing, statistics office or trade body figure, every step is sourced and no funnel's midpoint is more than 1.5 times another's; one assumed step, a weaker start or funnels further apart hold it at medium, and a start no growth rate carries to the reference year or two assumed steps in a funnel at low. A figure the figures table marks assessed, or one with no exchange rate into the scope's currency, cannot start a funnel. The note says in a sentence or two how the figures were carried to this scope.
- **A derived headline.** A derived base covering every scope country, at the reference year, takes `bridge`: the steps from the bottom-up total to its figure, one per adjustment, each with its amount, a label, its provenance and its source. A figure the user gave is a step with provenance `user`; a step you assessed yourself also carries its method in one line, how its amount was reached. The steps add up to the figure, or the tool refuses them; the page draws them as the walk from the bottom-up to the headline. Each step's label says in the reader's words why what it adds is not in the bottom-up already, or for a step that takes off why the bottom-up counts it when it should not, such as "Companies that report one Nordic total and no figure per country", never in the method's own shorthand. The base's note prints right under the walk's heading, so it answers the reader's first question about it: what the bottom-up cannot count and why, what the steps add back or take off, and which companies, if any, count only in the high case because their revenue is itself an estimate.
- **Companies the list misses.** A company the list holds never enters through a step: where its report gives only a wider region, it counts in the bottom-up through an estimated split in `group_country_splits`, so the segments, the largest companies and the shares count it too. A company the list does not hold, such as a foreign group with no local filing, enters the base through a derived headline step where a filing or a report gives its revenue in the scope countries, at the in-scope share the source gives or one you assess, which is then a swing. Where its revenue itself is only your estimate, it widens the high case alone and the page names it as a gap.
- **The remainder.** A geography the scope names that no enumeration reached, such as a "Rest of Europe", still needs a figure, or the total leaves it out; the tool lists it as missing. Size it on one basis: a publisher's figure for a region less that same publisher's figures for the named geographies inside it where one exists, otherwise a proxy such as construction output or population scaled from the named geographies' own figures. Pass it in `bases` with the label derived, `built_from_companies: false`, the method in the note and every input in `inputs`. Never take the top-down total less the bottom-up of the named countries: the two count different things, so the difference is their disagreement rather than a market.
- **Derivations.** Where the user asked for a split the sources do not publish, say a regional base by country, build it from a stated proxy (hospital beds, health spending, population, the bottom-up's own weights) and pass it in `derivations` with the method in one sentence and every input with its value and source. The parts of one split share one method, worded the same for each and naming none of their own numbers, which go in each part's inputs, so the page states the method once. Refusing the split is not the honest answer when a method can be stated; the honest answer is the split with its weights shown. Where the headline was chosen over the bottom-up because the bottom-up distorts, split it by a proxy that distortion does not reach, not by the bottom-up's own weights. Under a player list the bottom-up sits where each maker is headquartered, so the geographies are always derived this way.
- **The gap causes.** For each country with a leg, name in `gap_causes` the cause of its gap to the nearest leg, as the result prints it for that row. The page prints only the whole market's gap, in the overview, so the cause under `total` is the one the reader sees; a total gap you cannot explain goes on the page as a finding, with no cause named. With a top-down estimate the Overview sets each piece against its top-down instead and prints no gap cause, so leave `gap_causes` out.
- **Assumptions.** The page shows the swings as sliders, the scope choices above them and your other calls in a short list below. Pass in `assumptions` each scope choice the user made or confirmed, or that you made by a cell's rule during the run, that a reader would want beside the range (what is in, what is out, which revenue counts) marked `scope`, and each other call a reader would challenge that is not a swing and that no other input holds marked `estimate`: the question with its answer, what it moves the total by where that is a number, the source it was read from, who decided it, and why in the note. The page adds the pass-through share and the assessed growth rates itself, and the bases, derivations, check answers and corrections to a company or to revenue per employee show elsewhere on it, so leave those out. Add any other call on a single company only when it is among the run's largest.

Every note, cause and answer you pass prints on the page as written, so each sentence carries at most one figure; the inputs and the page's tables carry the rest. Any figure of the market or a part of it in text you pass, inputs and methods included, reads as the page prints it: money with its currency symbol, in bn where the headline is 3bn or more and in m below, with one decimal at most ("$3.3bn of $13.7bn", not "3,251m of 13,749m"), and its shares with one decimal at most. A per-company or per-unit amount, or a weight, keeps the unit and precision its arithmetic needs, such as "20 companies at €50m each". The tool's replies print whole millions for your arithmetic, so convert a figure before you quote it.

**The checks.** The tool compares the facts with each other and prints what disagrees, each check with its id and severity. It works out which market each figure measures from what the helpers recorded it counting, so a figure that only overlaps the scope bounds nothing. Answer every high one in `check_answers`: with its cause where the records show it, otherwise start it with "Unresolved:", saying what you checked and what would settle it. An unresolved check is a finding and never holds up the range. Each check goes on the page as it stands, with the answer it was given; one that touches a base is marked as on the headline figure, and the ledes state it as plainly as the tool does.

**The base case** is the reconciliation's headline: net where a factor with provenance exists and the scope asked for net, gross otherwise. To build the low and high cases, rank the assumptions by swing: how much the total moves when the assumption travels across its plausible range. Swing is not the same as contribution; a large input with a narrow plausible range moves the total less than a small input nobody can pin down. The low and high cases move the base by the largest swings and name the assumptions that drove them. The swings are your judgement, not the tool's arithmetic: one whose ends a source or the user set carries that provenance, the rest are assessed. Shares the checks flag as resting on a company description alone, the tail's low and high, the unplaced foreign groups, the country splits estimated from a wider region, the home-country companies whose split could not be read, the companies without a share and the spread between publishers are the first candidates. Pass the result in `range`, with the note naming the assumptions that drive it, and each swing in `swings`, largest first: the assumption, what it measures, its low, base and high in its own unit, the total with it at either end, and your assessment in a sentence with its source where one exists. Each swing moves the total away from the headline, and together their moves land on the range's low and high, or the tool refuses them.

**The headline is the bottom-up, and the top-down is its check.** Set the two side by side piece by piece and say in the ledes where they part and why. Where the bottom-up carries a gross headline and half or more of the companies' revenue figures are estimates, the top-down carries 30% of the headline on its own; no blend is made where a published figure carries part of the headline, the headline is net of pass-through, it leaves out a geography the top-down counts, a piece has no report or a start no growth rate carries to the reference year, or research placed too little revenue in segments; the tool names which, and a weight the user asked for is then reported back to them as not possible. Pass `headline_weight` only when the user asks for a weight, with their request as its reason, and 0 when they want the top-down left out. A figure for a wider definition than the scope reaches the scope through a funnel; on its own it is never the high case, which is this scope with its assumptions at the top of their plausible range.

**Settling it.** The user is not asked here. Before any range is written, settle the reconciliation's open questions by the rules above: each high check, which figure carries the headline and which countries or regions a base carries, the top-down estimate, how a remainder is sized, whether to split a regional base by a proxy, the framing of the range. Then call `grasp_reconcile_market_size` again with every judgement input, your choices and the range among them, since each call replaces the last; the page is drawn from that record. The bases chosen can raise high checks that were not there before; if the call refuses the range over one, answer it and call again.
