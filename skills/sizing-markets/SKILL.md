# Sizing Markets

A market sizing here is a range that a due-diligence reader can defend, with the work shown: which figures came from filings and which pages, which were assumed and why, where the different routes to the number disagree and what would settle it. Where the company universe can be enumerated, the bottom-up over it produces the number and the top-down supplies the anchors, the proxies and the cross-checks. Where it cannot, which the scoping session decides, the top-down carries the number and the bottom-up sizes the countries it can reach.

The method runs as a fixed sequence of tools. Each writes a record for this sizing's `conversation_id`, and the next tool reads it by itself. A tool called out of order refuses and names the record it is missing; that is the method, not an error to work around. Grasp's own helpers do all the reading of reports and web pages, so you never search the web for this and never pass them a figure you found yourself, unless the user gave it to you.

Call `grasp_setup_conversation` once at the start and use its `conversation_id` for every tool in the sizing and for every table the sizing builds. If the sizing tools are not available, market research is not part of this organization's Grasp plan: say so instead of estimating the market another way.

Each phase's instructions are a separate read. When the table below says to read `skill:sizing-markets/<part>`, call the `grasp_read_docs` tool with that as its `query` before doing anything else in the phase. Their rules bind as much as the ones here.

| Phase | What you do |
| ----- | ----------- |
| 1 | Read `skill:sizing-markets/scoping`. Draw the grid, propose defaults, confirm in chat, the run's only pause, then call `grasp_set_market_sizing_scope`. |
| 2 | Read `skill:sizing-markets/bottom-up`, which covers phases 2 and 3. Start the research first: call `grasp_research_market_size_top_down` and `grasp_research_market_growth` together. Each starts a job of up to 20 minutes, one helper reading the reports into the handover record and a second writing the growth record. Don't wait for them. Build the universe table, call `grasp_check_market_data_coverage` and tell the user how much of the number will be measured. |
| 3 | Call `grasp_research_market_size_bottom_up`: the attribution pass and its audit, one job of 10 to 30 minutes that writes the audit record. It waits for the research to finish where it needs it. |
| 4 | Read `skill:sizing-markets/reconcile`. Call `grasp_reconcile_market_size`, settle its open questions by its rules, then call it again with your choices and the range. |
| 5 | Read `skill:sizing-markets/output`. Call `grasp_write_sizing_page` and share the link it returns. |

## Jobs

The three research tools return a `job_id` at once. Poll with `grasp_check_job_status`, about once a minute as its `next_poll_after_seconds` says. A finished job's text is the step's whole reply, so read it there. Poll the bottom-up; you need not poll the top-down and growth jobs while it runs, because it waits for their records and repeats their summaries. Check each of them once when it has finished.

If you have to stop polling, tell the user the job carries on without you, say what it is doing, and ask them to say "check again"; then check the same job rather than starting a new one. The jobs save their progress as they go.

Calling a research tool again continues from the last record on the same scope, adding to what the earlier call found. A failed job or a refusal that tells you to call again needs a new `idempotency_key`, or you get the same job back. A job that fails without saying what to do gets one more try with a new `idempotency_key`; what it filled is kept. If that fails too, it is a stop. A tool that says a job for its record is already running means wait for it.

## Rules that hold in every phase

- **No market figure before the grid is confirmed.** Reading a named company's profile, or a page on how the industry segments itself, is fine. A number is not.
- **The run moves toward a shape, not one number.** For the whole market and for every geography the scope reports and every offering the grid fixed: a size today, five years back and five forward, and the drivers behind the movement, tailwinds and headwinds, with how much each matters and which part of the market it lands on. A part with none of that is a recorded gap on the page, never a silence. One growth rate quoted across the whole market, with the trends as a list of sentences, is the outcome this method exists to avoid.
- **The bottom-up is never set aside for the top-down.** A universe whose raw sum double-counts or is contaminated is consolidated and cleaned inside `grasp_research_market_size_bottom_up`; that is the method working. A top-down figure carries a country's or a region's number, passed to `grasp_reconcile_market_size` in `bases`, only where the scoping session left the country out of the enumeration, a statistics office or regulator publishes a figure for exactly the scope there, it sizes a remainder, or the user asks for it. Otherwise an enumerated country stays with its companies however much of it is modelled, and so does the headline under a player list. A failed tool call that names nothing to call next is a stop: report the error, ask the user how to proceed, and write no total from the top-down or from a raw sum of the table in the meantime.
- **Independence.** A leg calibrated to another leg, or a factor obtained by dividing one leg by another, is a cross-check of basis and nothing more. Say which legs are independent, and what share of the bottom-up rests on proxies taken from the anchors.
- **Agreement language.** A modelled figure that lands on another figure is "consistent with" it.
- **Every figure carries its source, its label and who published it**: claimed, third-party, derived, or assessed, and a credibility mark from filing to teaser. An assessed figure never enters a total or a headline. A figure you derive by a stated method goes to `grasp_reconcile_market_size` as a derivation. A figure the tools report as unread or as a lead is unread: it appears on the page as a gap, never as a citation. A document is cited by the exact file path a tool reply prints; the page refuses a source the conversation does not hold.
- **Provenance beside the headline.** Every judgement input to `grasp_reconcile_market_size` is sourced, the user's, or assumed with your reasoning. Assumed inputs come back as a block; the page lists them in its Assumptions section. When the reconciliation says a geography has no figure, settle it by the rules in phase 4 and record the choice. When the pass-through share has no source, the headline stays gross and the page names net as a gap.
- **One reference year, one currency, nominal terms**, fixed in the scope record. Neither is revisited after scoping; a filing from another year is restated by the tools.
- **Coverage, then audit, then total.** No total is quoted before `grasp_research_market_size_bottom_up` returns, and the market total comes from `grasp_reconcile_market_size` only. Quote it as its headline line prints it, the figure and its year together; moving a figure from one year to another is the tool's arithmetic, never yours.
- **Adjacent segments are reported separately**, never folded into the total.
- **Honest negatives are findings.** "This report quotes no market size" and "no independent figure exists for this country" belong on the page.
- **A scope boundary found wrong later is decided by the rule on its grid cell**, never by a silent reclassification: record it in `assumptions` as a scope choice you made, with the rule that decided it. If the decision changes the scope, call `grasp_set_market_sizing_scope` again; a later tool whose inputs changed says which records to rebuild, in order.

## Working rules

- **One pause, at scoping.** It is one message in chat, held in every run, with confirming the scope as the easy answer where nothing is open. Ask at most four questions, the ones the request left open that change the analysis most; every other default is stated in the message, not asked. Put your recommended option first and mark it recommended, and say what each option changes in the result. A question the user skips keeps its stated default and widens the range. A user who says to go ahead confirms the scope as stated. A user who says stop means stop.
- **After scoping, the choices are yours.** The user may be away until the page is done, so never stop to ask. Settle each open question by the rule the phase gives for it, and where none covers it, take the option a due-diligence reviewer would recommend from the evidence. Each choice is recorded where phase 4 says, with why you made it and the alternative. The only stops after scoping are a failed tool call that names nothing to call next, and a top-down or growth run still unfinished after two reruns.
- **Keep the user in the loop, at a high level.** Before each call that runs for minutes, one sentence on which phase starts and what it finds out. Name phases by what they do, never by tool name.
- **User-facing text.** Everything the user reads explains figures, gaps and choices through the market: its companies, sources and figures, not the tools, the table, rows or the pause. That means the scoping message, the closing message, and all text the page prints, check answers and gap causes included. The page is read by people who never saw the chat. Name the parts of the number in the page's words: top-down for a market size someone else published, bottom-up for the companies added up, companies with N or more employees and companies under N employees, where N is the size floor. Avoid published figure, tail, smaller firms, modelled and reconciliation.
- **Tables.** The universe lives in a table built with `grasp_create_table`, `grasp_compose_table` and `grasp_update_table`; every per-company judgement is a column the tools write. `grasp_query_table` is for inspecting rows, and it returns at most 50, so aggregate rather than list. Research and categorical columns hold text, so compare them with their labels (`= 'in'`), never as booleans.
- **Corrections.** A figure the user supplies, or a split a later read finds, goes back into `grasp_research_market_size_bottom_up` as a correction with its source. A user-supplied figure names the user in its `source`, which is how the page attributes it.
- **Currency.** Use the currency the user works in; ask only if you can't tell and the market spans several. The scope record fixes the sizing currency once.
- **Follow-up questions after the page.** Answer in a short paragraph or a small table: the answer with its source first, then at most the one reason behind it. Leave out the calculation steps and caveats unless asked; offer them in one line instead.
- **Follow-ups that change a figure.** When the user corrects a figure or the scope, a table edit changes which companies the market counts, or your own answer finds a figure on the page wrong, rerun the tool whose record it changes and each one after it, and redraw the page with `grasp_write_sizing_page` in the same turn without asking. A question about a figure the method estimates by design, such as a share or a remainder, is answered, not rerun. A shortlist or other working view of the counted companies goes in a new table built from theirs, so the table the page counts keeps its rows. Where the rerun needs research the user did not ask for, such as a new scope, say in one line that the page now differs and offer it instead. Never work out a page figure in chat or code: what the tools cannot produce is answered in chat, labelled as your estimate, and kept off the page.
