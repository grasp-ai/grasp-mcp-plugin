## Phase 5: the page

The sizing ends in one HTML page that `grasp_write_sizing_page` draws from the records. It returns a link that opens the page in a browser and expires after 7 days, and a link to the same page in Grasp that does not expire and needs a Grasp sign-in. Share both as they are, and offer to write the page again for a fresh browser link when one has run out. The page's links to PDFs, the company table and company profiles open in Grasp too. The tool draws every standard section itself: the headline with its range and what carries it, the size chart with its growth either side, the top-down figures beside the bottom-up, the split by country or region, the drivers and the bridge from today's market to the forecast year, the assumptions beside the headline, the largest companies, the coverage and the cleaning, the gaps and the checks between the facts, and the sources with every figure read.

**Before you call it.** `grasp_reconcile_market_size` has been called with the open questions settled: the bases, the range with the swings that drive it, and any derivation you built. Without a range the page says that no low and high case were set, so a page written from an earlier reconciliation shows the gap rather than hiding it. Read that reconciliation output once more; the text you write describes it.

**What you write.** Everything below prints on the page as you write it, beside tables that already hold the numbers. A sentence carries at most one figure; a point that needs several is a panel's table or list, or is left to the section's own tables.

- **Title, in scope, out of scope.** The name of the market, with no country or company count; the scope record in words, offerings and the value-chain layer in, adjacent cells out. Basis, reference year and currency are drawn from the record; do not repeat them.
- **One lede per section** but the sources, one or two sentences on what the reader should take from it: how the top-down and bottom-up figures compare, without restating the headline and range the summary carries, what the breakdowns show, what moves the market, what the range turns on, how much of the bottom-up is measured, what is missing. A lede quotes only a figure the reconciliation printed, written as the page prints it ("€4.2bn", not "4,187m"), and a count of companies or sources is a figure: it takes the number the records give, not a rounded or recounted one. A lede states what the figures show and does not characterise the market beyond them. Write for a partner reading an investment memo: the finding first, short sentences, plain words. The page's own labels carry the method and mark which figure carries the total and each geography, so a lede needs neither.
- **Stories.** For each geography the page reports, its market's story in one sentence: where it went and why, as its drivers and rates show it. A market with one geography takes one.
- **Gaps.** Data gaps ranked by their effect on the total, each a short headline with a note on how it moves the number: the figure it sits under, which way it pushes it and roughly how far. The growth search's gaps, the checks between the facts and the sources nobody could read are appended from the records; do not repeat them. A source behind a paywall is listed as one; never advise buying it.
- **Expert questions**, aimed at the assumptions that move the range, each as the question itself with who to ask beside it: a role and a company or a kind of company.
- **Panels** this market needs that the standard sections do not draw, as typed rows: a key-value list, a table, horizontal bars or a stacked bar. A split by end market or by offering the growth record holds shares for; the in-scope share by company type. Every figure in a panel is one a tool returned, with its label where the panel kind takes one. A figure no tool returned is not a panel; it is a gap. The subtitle says what the numbers are, their unit and year; a panel read from one document cites it in source. Bars that split one total, such as the market by offering, set parts_of_a_whole so each prints its share. A panel never redraws what a standard section already shows, such as the largest companies or the split by country, under another title or with a column added; something that table lacks reaches it through its record, as a figure does, or is a gap.
- **Sources.** One entry for every document `grasp_reconcile_market_size` lists as cited and every document your panels cite, so a reader sees what each one is before opening it: its title as the document gives it, and its kind with its year where it has one, such as "Annual report 2025", "Industry report", "Government budget 2026" or "Press release". Take both from what you read, not from the file name. The tool refuses a page that leaves a cited document out.

**Afterwards.** The closing message is the result and its pitch, not the method. Open with the headline as the reconciliation's headline line prints it, with its range. Then the two or three findings a partner would repeat in a meeting: where the market's weight sits, where it is heading and what drives it, and the assumption the number turns on. Then the choices you made after scoping that move the number most, at most three, each in one line with why and what it would take to change it. Then give the page's link. How the number was built and its tables stay on the page. A caveat earns a place only as what it means for the number, such as a figure that may sit high or low and why. Offer at most three next steps, ones the run has not taken: check each against the reconciliation before offering it. End by inviting the user to challenge any figure, share, driver or assumption on the page, or to ask for any part of it to go deeper.

To change the page's wording, call `grasp_write_sizing_page` again with the new words; it redraws the page, and the figures come fresh from the records. A figure the user wants changed goes back through the tools, as a correction to `grasp_research_market_size_bottom_up` or a base to `grasp_reconcile_market_size`, and the page is redrawn; never offer to edit a figure on the page alone.
