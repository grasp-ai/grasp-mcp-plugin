## Phase 2: research, universe and coverage

The bottom-up produces the number: a market figure that resolves to named companies with filed revenue, plus an estimated tail. Every per-company judgement it makes becomes a column on the table, so the work can be inspected row by row.

Start `grasp_research_market_size_top_down` and `grasp_research_market_growth` first and in the same step. Pass `focus` to the growth search when the user has said what they want from the trends, in their own words: the drivers that matter to them, a region they want the growth split by, a kind of source they trust. It is also how you act on such a request after the first search.

**Building the universe.** The scope record is applied as confirmed, the HQ rule included. What the universe then shows, say foreign groups' global revenue in the top band or a trade that caps, is handled by the attribution pass or placed by the rule on its grid cell; it never changes a confirmed setting in place. The scoping answers settle the HQ rule and every other alignment question for each search, filter and research column in the run, a later wider search included; do not ask again. Run one `grasp_create_table` search per offering row of the grid, per country the scope enumerates, passing the HQ rule as `geography.include_multinational_companies`. Wait for each with `grasp_check_job_status`, then combine them into one table with `grasp_compose_table`, in the same `conversation_id`. A search returns at most about 10,000 rows, and the size floor cannot be applied at search time, so a broad trade in a large country will hit that cap. When it does, split the trade into the sub-trades the industry itself uses and search each. A sub-trade that still caps is a sample rather than the universe: the page lists it as a gap. For a player list, search each offering across the whole geography rather than per country, and add a second search for the wider category this market sits in, so the groups that sell a line in it are in the table too. Compose that search with `filter_sql: "COALESCE(reported_employees, linkedin_employees) >= 50"`: its smaller rows are mostly makers of the wider category rather than of this market, and counted one by one they inflate both the core and the tail.

**Columns.** The standard company table already carries revenue, consolidated accounts, headcount, country, description and products. Add, with `grasp_update_table` and `workflow.data_columns.add`: `year_of_revenue_last_available`, `financial_source`, `estimated_revenue_<ccy>`, `global_ultimate_owner`, and `revenue_last_available_year_<ccy>` when the sizing currency is not USD. Add them on each component search rather than after combining; a composed table carries its components' data columns, and `grasp_research_market_size_bottom_up` names any it is still missing before it runs. The `ownership` output stays deferred; the attribution pass consolidates on `global_ultimate_owner`.

**Headcount and the floor.** Headcount is the reported headcount, else LinkedIn's, and a zero in either counts as unknown. A company with no staff on record that files at least what a company at the floor would earn, as a holding filing its group's revenue does, is researched and counted like any other filer without a headcount; one that files less stays in the tail. Keep every row in the table, including the ones below the floor: they feed the tail estimate. Never set a saved view filter on this table; a saved filter is applied to every later query, and the rows it hides are exactly the ones the tail and the coverage count need.

**Coverage before any sum.** Call `grasp_check_market_data_coverage` with the `table_id`. Nothing is put to the user here and no setting changes: the scope's floor and reference year stand, so nothing reruns before the companies are sized. Tell the user in one sentence how much of the eventual number will be measured and how much modelled, then go on to the attribution. A large count of companies with neither revenue nor headcount is a gap on the page. A country more than half modelled stays with its companies unless a statistics office or regulator publishes a figure for exactly the scope there: those with only a headcount are sized at their peers' revenue per employee, or at one a source gives, and the range widens over them. A foreign group's revenue in a scope country is taken from its local subsidiaries' filings where they file, passed as corrections; the rest is left to the report readers and is a gap where they cannot place it. A boundary decision that changes the scope is written with `grasp_set_market_sizing_scope` again before the attribution runs, and once it has returned, the research it reruns is started again, before the attribution, so it works alongside while the attribution researches the companies.

## Phase 3: the attribution pass

The attribution pass is one job: `grasp_research_market_size_bottom_up` with the `table_id`. It restates each filing to the reference year at the growth record's rate for that country over the year into the reference year, researches and computes each row's scope classification, in-scope share and revenue, consolidates parents and subsidiaries, and estimates the tail. Then it audits: the revenue-per-employee outliers and the group-scale filings in the tail are researched from their filings; the largest groups without a disclosed split (foreign ones, and those based in the scope that own other rows) and the three largest companies without one that are based in the scope and owned by no other company in the table each get a reader of their reports for their country split; and rows headquartered outside the scope with no in-scope share are skipped. After the readers, the companies the total hangs on get an attribution review: the largest contributors until most of the core is covered, the groups so large that one share point moves the total, and in-scope rows with a share but no revenue, each checked by a reader of its own for its filed revenue, whether another company already counts it, and its in-scope share with a band and a written motivation. The answers are applied in a recompute. Do not build the columns by hand and do not audit the rows yourself; the tool is the recipe and the audit.

**What comes back** in the finished job's text, in this order: coverage of the research, the consolidation, possible same-group duplicates, the outliers still held out after the audit, the top ten contributors, the totals per country and revenue basis for rows in the total, the in-scope revenue the core leaves out because no share could be attributed, the country-of-activity view with the foreign groups the readers could not place, the tail with its range, the restated share, gross, then the audit tables: the verdict on every contested filing, the outcome of every report reader, and every attribution review with the first pass beside it.

**What you do with it.** Read the gap lines and carry them onto the page with their amounts: outliers still held out, companies without a share, foreign groups not placed, and companies based in the scope whose split could not be read, which are counted whole in their home country. When the gross lands well below the nearest published figure for the same scope, look for who is missing before accepting the gap: the larger groups with a small line in this market, and the players the reports name. Add them with a wider search, call `grasp_research_market_size_bottom_up` again, and say what the addition moved; a gap that remains is then a finding rather than a list that stopped early. A company whose share or revenue still looks wrong after the review, or a double count you suspect, goes in `review_add` with the reason, and the next call reviews it. Shares you estimate for the companies counted at 0%, as the result asks, are one of the range's swings, low with them at the bottom of their plausible range, high at the top. Where the user gave a figure at scoping that settles one of them, or a boundary you decided moves a company, call `grasp_research_market_size_bottom_up` again with the correction in `group_country_splits`, `share_overrides`, `revenue_overrides` or `rpe_overrides` and its source. A country with too few filers is sized from the floor band across the scope, and the tail table names that source; a revenue per employee a source gives for a country more than half modelled goes in `rpe_overrides`. A country the tail table leaves without an estimate is a gap, never a zero: the reconciliation does not repeat it, so size its companies under the floor at their peers' revenue per employee through `rpe_overrides` and call again; where no figure can be stated, it is a gap and one of the range's swings.
